Vocabulary lesson 1. Words 1 to 20.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 1. Opaque.
Meaning: Not clear; hard to understand.
First example: The instructions were opaque, so I couldn’t understand them.
Second example: His explanation was too opaque for beginners.

Word 2. Hands-on.
Meaning: Actively involved in doing something.
First example: I prefer a hands-on approach to learning.
Second example: Our manager is very hands-on with the team.

Word 3. Almost certainly.
Meaning: Very likely; nearly sure.
First example: It will almost certainly rain today.
Second example: He will almost certainly win the match.

Word 4. Whack.
Meaning: Hit strongly / try something quickly.
First example: He whacked the ball across the field.
Second example: Let me take a whack at solving this problem.

Word 5. Pause and ponder.
Meaning: Stop and think carefully.
First example: Sometimes you should pause and ponder before making decisions.
Second example: I had to pause and ponder the question.

Word 6. Dissipate.
Meaning: Gradually disappear or fade away.
First example: My anger dissipated after some time.
Second example: The fog dissipated in the morning sunlight.

Word 7. Erratic.
Meaning: Unpredictable; not regular.
First example: His work schedule is very erratic.
Second example: The internet connection is erratic today.

Word 8. Constraints.
Meaning: Limits or restrictions.
First example: We finished the project despite time constraints.
Second example: Budget constraints affect our plans.

Word 9. Abrasive.
Meaning: Rude or harsh in behavior.
First example: His abrasive tone upset everyone.
Second example: She can be a bit abrasive when stressed.

Word 10. Dysfunctional.
Meaning: Not working properly.
First example: The team became dysfunctional due to poor communication.
Second example: Their management system is completely dysfunctional.

Word 11. Peers.
Meaning: People of the same age or status.
First example: We learn a lot from our peers.
Second example: He is respected by his peers.

Word 12. Abundance.
Meaning: A very large amount.
First example: There is an abundance of opportunities online.
Second example: The garden has an abundance of flowers.

Word 13. Low-key.
Meaning: Simple, quiet, not flashy.
First example: We had a low-key celebration at home.
Second example: He kept the event low-key.

Word 14. Crummy.
Meaning: Very bad or poor quality.
First example: The service at that hotel was crummy.
Second example: I had a crummy day at work.

Word 15. Nagged.
Meaning: Repeatedly complained or reminded someone.
First example: My mother nagged me to clean my room.
Second example: He felt nagged by constant questions.

Word 16. Gloomy.
Meaning: Dark, sad, or depressing.
First example: The weather looks gloomy today.
Second example: He felt gloomy after hearing the bad news.

Word 17. Lunatic fringe view.
Meaning: Extreme or crazy opinion held by a small group.
First example: Some people still believe that lunatic fringe views online.
Second example: That idea sounds like a lunatic fringe view to most scientists.

Word 18. Equivalent.
Meaning: Equal in value or meaning.
First example: One kilometer is equivalent to 1000 meters.
Second example: This job experience is equivalent to a degree.

Word 19. Potbelly stove.
Meaning: A round metal stove used for heating rooms.
First example: Old houses used potbelly stoves for warmth.
Second example: The cabin had a small potbelly stove in the corner.

Word 20. Partly.
Meaning: To some degree, but not completely.
First example: I was partly responsible for the mistake.
Second example: The road is partly closed today.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

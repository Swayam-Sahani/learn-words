Vocabulary lesson 9. Words 161 to 180.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 161. Straighten out.
Meaning: Fix or solve a problem.
First example: We need to straighten out this misunderstanding.
Second example: Let’s straighten out the issue before it grows bigger.

Word 162. Look me dead in the eyes.
Meaning: Look directly and honestly at someone.
First example: He looked me dead in the eyes and told the truth.
Second example: If you believe it, look me dead in the eyes and say it.

Word 163. More or less.
Meaning: Approximately or almost.
First example: I’m more or less finished with the work.
Second example: The plan is more or less ready.

Word 164. Feud.
Meaning: Long-lasting conflict between people.
First example: The families had a feud for many years.
Second example: Their business feud became famous.

Word 165. Bump into.
Meaning: Meet someone unexpectedly.
First example: I bumped into an old friend yesterday.
Second example: You never know who you might bump into in this city.

Word 166. Ok chill.
Meaning: Relax or calm down.
First example: Okay, chill, it’s not a big problem.
Second example: Just chill, everything will work out.

Word 167. Cheeky.
Meaning: Slightly rude but playful.
First example: He made a cheeky comment.
Second example: That was a cheeky joke.

Word 168. Demean me.
Meaning: Make someone feel less important or humiliated.
First example: Please don’t demean me in front of others.
Second example: His words seemed to demean her.

Word 169. Mutt.
Meaning: A mixed-breed dog (sometimes used jokingly for a person).
First example: That mutt is very friendly.
Second example: He jokingly called himself a mutt.

Word 170. Pebble.
Meaning: A small stone.
First example: I threw a pebble into the lake.
Second example: My shoe had a small pebble in it.

Word 171. Get off.
Meaning: Leave or step away from something.
First example: Please get off the bus at the next stop.
Second example: Get off the grass.

Word 172. In the spirit of.
Meaning: Done with the intention of a certain feeling or purpose.
First example: In the spirit of teamwork, let’s help each other.
Second example: In the spirit of learning, mistakes are okay.

Word 173. Absolute reverence.
Meaning: Deep respect and admiration.
First example: People speak about the leader with absolute reverence.
Second example: The temple is treated with absolute reverence.

Word 174. Phenomenal.
Meaning: Extremely good or impressive.
First example: The performance was phenomenal.
Second example: She achieved phenomenal success in business.

Word 175. Perceive.
Meaning: To notice or understand something.
First example: People perceive situations differently.
Second example: I perceive this problem in another way.

Word 176. Utterly.
Meaning: Completely; totally.
First example: I was utterly shocked by the news.
Second example: The idea is utterly unrealistic.

Word 177. Relieved.
Meaning: Feeling relaxed after worry or stress.
First example: I felt relieved after finishing the exam.
Second example: She looked relieved when the problem was solved.

Word 178. Tremendous.
Meaning: Very great or impressive.
First example: He made tremendous progress this year.
Second example: The team showed tremendous effort.

Word 179. Lien.
Meaning: Legal right to keep someone’s property until a debt is paid.
First example: The bank placed a lien on the property.
Second example: The contractor filed a lien for unpaid work.

Word 180. Overwhelmed.
Meaning: Feeling too much pressure or emotion.
First example: I felt overwhelmed with all the work.
Second example: She was overwhelmed by the support she received.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 2. Words 21 to 40.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 21. Virtue.
Meaning: A good moral quality.
First example: Honesty is a great virtue.
Second example: Patience is an important virtue in business.

Word 22. Nevertheless.
Meaning: In spite of that.
First example: The work was difficult; nevertheless, we finished it.
Second example: It was raining; nevertheless, they continued the match.

Word 23. Nonetheless.
Meaning: Despite what was said before.
First example: The plan was risky; nonetheless, we tried it.
Second example: He was tired, nonetheless he kept working.

Word 24. Repertoire.
Meaning: A collection of skills or things someone can do.
First example: Communication is part of his professional repertoire.
Second example: She added new recipes to her cooking repertoire.

Word 25. Exponential.
Meaning: Increasing very rapidly.
First example: Technology is growing at an exponential rate.
Second example: The number of users increased exponentially.

Word 26. Linear.
Meaning: Growing in a straight, steady way.
First example: The company's growth has been linear this year.
Second example: Learning a language is rarely a linear process.

Word 27. Superiority complex.
Meaning: Feeling that you are better than others.
First example: He acts like he has a superiority complex.
Second example: A superiority complex can damage relationships.

Word 28. Inferiority.
Meaning: Feeling less capable or less important.
First example: He struggles with feelings of inferiority.
Second example: Don't let inferiority stop you from trying.

Word 29. Be courteous.
Meaning: Be polite and respectful.
First example: Always be courteous to customers.
Second example: It costs nothing to be courteous to others.

Word 30. Conversely.
Meaning: In the opposite way.
First example: Some people love working alone; conversely, others prefer teamwork.
Second example: Exercise improves health; conversely, inactivity harms it.

Word 31. Vice versa.
Meaning: The opposite is also true.
First example: Teachers learn from students and vice versa.
Second example: Good habits create success and vice versa.

Word 32. Thoroughly.
Meaning: Completely; very carefully.
First example: I thoroughly checked the report before sending it.
Second example: She thoroughly enjoyed the movie.

Word 33. Contemplate.
Meaning: Think deeply about something.
First example: I need time to contemplate this decision.
Second example: He sat quietly to contemplate his future plans.

Word 34. Shabby.
Meaning: Old, worn out, or in poor condition.
First example: He was wearing shabby clothes.
Second example: The hotel room looked shabby and poorly maintained.

Word 35. Chores.
Meaning: Small routine tasks at home.
First example: I finished my household chores early today.
Second example: Children should help with chores at home.

Word 36. Derogatory.
Meaning: Showing disrespect or insult.
First example: His derogatory comment upset everyone.
Second example: Avoid making derogatory remarks about others.

Word 37. Barren.
Meaning: Empty; unable to grow plants.
First example: The land was dry and barren.
Second example: Without creativity, ideas can become barren.

Word 38. Uncanny.
Meaning: Strange in a surprising way.
First example: He has an uncanny ability to remember names.
Second example: The twins look uncannily similar.

Word 39. Distinct.
Meaning: Clearly different or separate.
First example: There are distinct differences between the two plans.
Second example: Her voice is very distinct.

Word 40. Distinguish.
Meaning: Recognize the difference between things.
First example: It’s hard to distinguish truth from rumors.
Second example: Experience helps you distinguish good ideas from bad ones.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 22. Words 421 to 440.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 421. to grasp it.
Meaning: Understand something.
First example: I couldn't grasp what he was trying to say.
Second example: It took me some time to grasp the concept.
Third example: Once you grasp the basics, everything becomes easier.

Word 422. run short.
Meaning: Have less of something than needed.
First example: We're running short of milk.
Second example: We're running short of time to finish the project.
Third example: I ran short of money during my trip.

Word 423. barge in.
Meaning: Enter or interrupt rudely without permission.
First example: Don't barge into my room without knocking.
Second example: He barged into the meeting without permission.
Third example: Sorry to barge in, but I need to tell you something.

Word 424. shape perception.
Meaning: Influence how someone sees or understands something.
First example: Social media can shape our perception of beauty.
Second example: Branding helps shape customers' perception of a company.
Third example: Our childhood experiences shape our perception of the world.

Word 425. perceive.
Meaning: Understand, interpret or see something in a particular way.
First example: I perceive him as a trustworthy person.
Second example: Customers perceive this brand as premium.
Third example: People perceive failure differently.

Word 426. early on.
Meaning: At an early stage or in the beginning.
First example: I realized early on that something was wrong.
Second example: We identified the problem early on in the project.
Third example: You should develop good habits early on in life.

Word 427. nerve of steel.
Meaning: Being extremely brave and calm under pressure.
First example: You need nerves of steel to do that job.
Second example: She handled the client crisis with nerves of steel.
Third example: The firefighter showed nerves of steel during the rescue.

Word 428. whole life flash just before my eyes.
Meaning: I felt extremely scared, as if I might die.
First example: When the car almost hit me, my whole life flashed before my eyes.
Second example: When I accidentally deleted the project files, my whole life flashed before my eyes.
Third example: I slipped from the stairs, and my whole life flashed before my eyes.

Word 429. not cutout to be.
Meaning: Not naturally suited for something.
First example: I'm not cut out to be a doctor.
Second example: I don't think I'm cut out to be a salesperson.
Third example: Maybe I'm not cut out to live in a big city.

Word 430. worn out.
Meaning: Extremely tired or damaged from use.
First example: I'm completely worn out after work.
Second example: These laptops are worn out and need replacing.
Third example: My shoes are getting worn out.

Word 431. buzzkill.
Meaning: Someone or something that ruins the fun.
First example: Don't be such a buzzkill!
Second example: Cancelling the event at the last minute was a real buzzkill.
Third example: The rain was a total buzzkill for our picnic.

Word 432. to get there.
Meaning: To reach a goal or desired result.
First example: I'm not perfect at English, but I'll get there.
Second example: We're making progress; we'll get there eventually.
Third example: It takes time to build a successful business, but you'll get there.

Word 433. pry about.
Meaning: Ask intrusive questions or interfere in someone's private matters.
First example: Stop prying into my personal life.
Second example: We shouldn't pry into the client's private matters.
Third example: He's always prying into other people's business.

Word 434. overkill.
Meaning: More than necessary; excessive.
First example: Buying five phones is a bit of an overkill.
Second example: Spending ₹1 lakh on a simple website would be overkill.
Third example: Wearing a suit to a casual meeting is a bit of an overkill.

Word 435. let me off the leash.
Meaning: Allow me more freedom to act.
First example: Just let me off the leash and I'll show you what I can do.
Second example: Give me more responsibility and let me off the leash.
Third example: My parents finally let me off the leash when I turned 18.

Word 436. arms are busted up.
Meaning: My arms are badly hurt or injured (informal).
First example: My arms are busted up after that workout.
Second example: He couldn't work because his arms were busted up.
Third example: I fell off my bike, and both my arms were pretty busted up.

Word 437. can pull this off.
Meaning: Can successfully do something difficult.
First example: I think I can pull this off.
Second example: We have limited time, but I believe we can pull this project off.
Third example: Nobody thought he could win, but he pulled it off.

Word 438. its turned out to be.
Meaning: Something has developed into an unexpected result.
First example: It's turned out to be a great day.
Second example: The project has turned out to be more complicated than expected.
Third example: What started as a small idea has turned out to be a successful business.

Word 439. beat to the pulp.
Meaning: Beat someone very badly.
First example: He was beaten to a pulp in the fight.
Second example: The boxer was beaten to a pulp during the match.
Third example: The old punching bag was battered almost to a pulp.

Word 440. fanatic.
Meaning: Someone extremely enthusiastic or obsessed about something.
First example: He's a fitness fanatic.
Second example: She's a technology fanatic and loves trying new tools.
Third example: I'm a cricket fanatic.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 8. Words 141 to 160.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 141. Fact of the matter.
Meaning: The real truth of a situation.
First example: The fact of the matter is that we need more time.
Second example: The fact of the matter is that success requires effort.

Word 142. Monopoly.
Meaning: When one company controls a market.
First example: The company has a monopoly in that industry.
Second example: Governments often regulate monopolies.

Word 143. Anomaly (correct spelling).
Meaning: Something unusual or different from normal.
First example: The sudden drop in sales was an anomaly.
Second example: Scientists noticed an anomaly in the data.

Word 144. Procession.
Meaning: A group of people moving together in a formal way.
First example: The wedding procession moved slowly through the street.
Second example: A procession followed the ceremony.

Word 145. Certainty (correct spelling).
Meaning: Being completely sure about something.
First example: There is no certainty about the future.
Second example: She spoke with great certainty.

Word 146. Revelation.
Meaning: A surprising discovery or realization.
First example: The documentary was a revelation for me.
Second example: It was a shocking revelation.

Word 147. Spontaneous.
Meaning: Done naturally without planning.
First example: The trip was completely spontaneous.
Second example: His reaction was spontaneous.

Word 148. Perhaps.
Meaning: Maybe; possibly.
First example: Perhaps we should try another approach.
Second example: Perhaps he will join us later.

Word 149. Pose yourself (a question).
Meaning: Ask yourself something to think deeply.
First example: Pose yourself an important question: what do you really want?
Second example: Sometimes you should pose yourself difficult questions.

Word 150. Revelatory and critical.
Meaning: Revealing important truth and analyzing it.
First example: The book is both revelatory and critical of society.
Second example: His speech was revelatory and critical.

Word 151. Manifest.
Meaning: To show or make something clear.
First example: His confidence manifested in his actions.
Second example: Success often manifests through consistent effort.

Word 152. Other way around.
Meaning: The opposite order or direction.
First example: I thought he called me, but it was the other way around.
Second example: Cause and effect sometimes work the other way around.

Word 153. Got humbled by.
Meaning: Became modest after realizing limits.
First example: He got humbled by the tough competition.
Second example: The experience humbled him.

Word 154. Pressure was on.
Meaning: A situation where expectations are high.
First example: In the final match, the pressure was on.
Second example: When the deadline came closer, the pressure was on.

Word 155. In a bit of a pickle.
Meaning: In a difficult or tricky situation.
First example: I’m in a bit of a pickle with this project.
Second example: He got into a pickle after missing the deadline.

Word 156. Take someone to the glory land.
Meaning: To defeat someone completely or embarrass them badly.
First example: The champion took his opponent to the glory land in the match.
Second example: Our team took them to the glory land in the final game.

Word 157. Shenanigans.
Meaning: Silly or dishonest tricks or behavior.
First example: The kids were up to their usual shenanigans.
Second example: There were some political shenanigans behind the decision.

Word 158. Smug.
Meaning: Too pleased with yourself.
First example: He looked smug after winning the argument.
Second example: Don’t act smug just because you succeeded once.

Word 159. Condescending.
Meaning: Acting like you are better than others.
First example: His tone sounded condescending.
Second example: Nobody likes a condescending attitude.

Word 160. Desolation.
Meaning: Extreme emptiness or loneliness.
First example: The abandoned town looked full of desolation.
Second example: He felt a sense of desolation after the loss.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 10. Words 181 to 200.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 181. Kicked in.
Meaning: Started to take effect.
First example: The medicine kicked in after an hour.
Second example: His survival instinct kicked in during the crisis.

Word 182. Moved away.
Meaning: Went to live somewhere else.
First example: My friend moved away to another city.
Second example: Many families moved away for better jobs.

Word 183. It’s high time.
Meaning: Something should have happened already.
First example: It’s high time we fixed this problem.
Second example: It’s high time you started taking your goals seriously.

Word 184. Up till now.
Meaning: Until this moment.
First example: Up till now, everything has been smooth.
Second example: Up till now, I haven't faced any issues.

Word 185. Retrospect (correct spelling).
Meaning: Thinking back about the past.
First example: In retrospect, I should have made a better decision.
Second example: In retrospect, the mistake taught me a lot.

Word 186. In order to.
Meaning: For the purpose of.
First example: I study daily in order to improve my skills.
Second example: We need discipline in order to succeed.

Word 187. Complete garbage.
Meaning: Something totally useless or bad.
First example: That explanation is complete garbage.
Second example: The rumor turned out to be complete garbage.

Word 188. Get an edge.
Meaning: Gain an advantage.
First example: Learning new skills helps you get an edge in business.
Second example: Practice helps athletes get an edge over competitors.

Word 189. Forte.
Meaning: Something you are very good at.
First example: Communication is his forte.
Second example: Marketing strategy is her forte.

Word 190. As of now.
Meaning: At the present time.
First example: As of now, I don’t have any updates.
Second example: As of now, the plan is still the same.

Word 191. Eternity.
Meaning: Forever; endless time.
First example: The meeting felt like an eternity.
Second example: Waiting for the results felt like eternity.

Word 192. Irony.
Meaning: A situation opposite to what you expect.
First example: It’s ironic that the fire station caught fire.
Second example: The irony is he teaches discipline but is always late.

Word 193. Devastating.
Meaning: Extremely damaging or shocking.
First example: The news was devastating for the whole family.
Second example: Losing the match like that was devastating.

Word 194. Stirring.
Meaning: Causing strong emotions or excitement.
First example: The speech was very stirring.
Second example: It was a stirring story about courage.

Word 195. Flabbergasted.
Meaning: Extremely surprised or shocked.
First example: I was flabbergasted when I heard the price.
Second example: She was flabbergasted by his sudden decision.

Word 196. Pulled out.
Meaning: To withdraw or leave something.
First example: The company pulled out of the deal.
Second example: He pulled out of the competition due to injury.

Word 197. Meddling.
Meaning: Interfering in someone else's matters.
First example: Stop meddling in my personal life.
Second example: I don’t like people meddling in my decisions.

Word 198. Backlash.
Meaning: Strong negative reaction from people.
First example: The decision caused a huge backlash online.
Second example: The company faced backlash from customers.

Word 199. Relinquish.
Meaning: To give up control or possession.
First example: He refused to relinquish his position.
Second example: She finally relinquished control of the project.

Word 200. Harboring.
Meaning: Keeping a feeling or secret inside.
First example: He is harboring anger towards his boss.
Second example: She has been harboring doubts about the plan.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

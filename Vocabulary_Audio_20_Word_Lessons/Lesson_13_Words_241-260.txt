Vocabulary lesson 13. Words 241 to 260.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 241. Expecting them.
Meaning: Waiting for someone to arrive.
First example: I’m expecting them around 7 pm.
Second example: We were expecting them earlier today.

Word 242. Sentiment analysis.
Meaning: Analyzing opinions or emotions in text using AI.
First example: Companies use sentiment analysis to understand customer feedback.
Second example: The tool performs sentiment analysis on social media posts.

Word 243. Distillation.
Meaning: Extracting the most important parts from something.
First example: The report is a distillation of months of research.
Second example: This summary is a distillation of the main ideas.

Word 244. Reciprocate.
Meaning: To return a feeling or action.
First example: She helped me, so I wanted to reciprocate.
Second example: If someone respects you, you should reciprocate.

Word 245. Empowered.
Meaning: Feeling confident and in control.
First example: Education makes people feel empowered.
Second example: She felt empowered after learning new skills.

Word 246. Extraordinary.
Meaning: Very unusual or impressive.
First example: She did an extraordinary job on the project.
Second example: The athlete showed extraordinary strength.

Word 247. Upstairs.
Meaning: On a higher floor of a building.
First example: My room is upstairs.
Second example: They went upstairs to talk privately.

Word 248. Downstairs.
Meaning: On a lower floor of a building.
First example: I left my phone downstairs.
Second example: Let’s go downstairs and have tea.

Word 249. Worked through.
Meaning: Managed to deal with or solve something.
First example: We worked through the problem together.
Second example: She worked through many challenges in her career.

Word 250. All day long.
Meaning: For the entire day.
First example: I was busy all day long.
Second example: It rained all day long yesterday.

Word 251. Create space.
Meaning: Make room or give time for something.
First example: I need to create space in my schedule.
Second example: Good leaders create space for new ideas.

Word 252. In the trenches.
Meaning: Doing the hard practical work.
First example: Entrepreneurs often work in the trenches early on.
Second example: The team in the trenches solved the issue quickly.

Word 253. Grew up in.
Meaning: Spent childhood in a place.
First example: I grew up in a small town.
Second example: She grew up in a business family.

Word 254. Shovel off.
Meaning: To remove something using a shovel.
First example: They shoveled off the snow from the road.
Second example: He had to shovel off the dirt from the path.

Word 255. Shoveling.
Meaning: Moving something like snow or dirt with a shovel.
First example: He spent hours shoveling snow.
Second example: They were shoveling sand into the truck.

Word 256. Trudge down.
Meaning: Walk slowly with difficulty or tiredness.
First example: I trudged down the street after a long day.
Second example: The workers trudged down the hill carrying tools.

Word 257. Hideous.
Meaning: Extremely ugly or unpleasant.
First example: The building looked hideous after the damage.
Second example: That color combination is hideous.

Word 258. Feathered hair.
Meaning: A hairstyle where hair is layered and brushed back like feathers.
First example: In the 80s many actors had feathered hair.
Second example: She styled her hair in a feathered look.

Word 259. Look like a troll.
Meaning: Look very messy or ugly (informal).
First example: I just woke up and look like a troll.
Second example: After the rain, I looked like a troll.

Word 260. Wrap it up.
Meaning: Finish something quickly.
First example: Let’s wrap it up; the meeting is over.
Second example: I need to wrap up this work before leaving.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

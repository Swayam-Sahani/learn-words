Vocabulary lesson 17. Words 321 to 340.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 321. Cultivate.
Meaning: To develop or improve a skill or quality.
First example: You should cultivate good habits.
Second example: Reading helps cultivate knowledge.

Word 322. Heralded.
Meaning: Announced or seen as a sign of something important.
First example: This project was heralded as a big success.
Second example: The invention was heralded as a breakthrough.

Word 323. Merely.
Meaning: Just or only.
First example: It was merely a misunderstanding.
Second example: He is not merely a worker; he is a leader.

Word 324. Vouch for.
Meaning: To confirm or support someone’s credibility.
First example: I can vouch for his honesty.
Second example: She vouched for her friend during the interview.

Word 325. Too good to be true.
Meaning: So good that it seems unrealistic.
First example: The offer sounded too good to be true.
Second example: His success story feels too good to be true.

Word 326. Point blank.
Meaning: Very directly and bluntly.
First example: She refused the offer point blank.
Second example: He asked me point blank about my plans.

Word 327. Long-term association.
Meaning: A relationship lasting for a long time.
First example: We are looking for a long-term association with clients.
Second example: Trust is important for a long-term association.

Word 328. Stammer.
Meaning: To speak with pauses or repeat sounds due to nervousness.
First example: I started to stammer during the interview.
Second example: He tends to stammer when he is stressed.

Word 329. Squeaked.
Meaning: Made a short, high-pitched sound.
First example: The door squeaked when I opened it.
Second example: She squeaked with excitement.

Word 330. Explicitly.
Meaning: Clearly and directly, without confusion.
First example: The manager explicitly told us to be on time.
Second example: Please explain the rules explicitly.

Word 331. Releasing tension.
Meaning: Letting go of stress or pressure.
First example: Exercise helps in releasing tension.
Second example: Talking to a friend is good for releasing tension.

Word 332. Capitulated.
Meaning: Gave up or surrendered after resistance.
First example: He finally capitulated and agreed to the plan.
Second example: The company capitulated after customer complaints.

Word 333. Show up.
Meaning: To arrive or appear.
First example: Make sure you show up on time.
Second example: He didn’t show up for the meeting.

Word 334. Blah blah blah.
Meaning: Used to describe boring or unimportant talk.
First example: He kept talking blah blah blah about politics.
Second example: I skipped the speech because it was just blah blah blah.

Word 335. Scrutinized.
Meaning: Examined very carefully.
First example: The teacher scrutinized my homework.
Second example: The police scrutinized the evidence.

Word 336. Hand knuckles.
Meaning: The joints of fingers.
First example: His hand knuckles turned white from gripping tightly.
Second example: I hurt my knuckles while lifting boxes.

Word 337. Agree to disagree.
Meaning: Accept that people have different opinions.
First example: We decided to agree to disagree.
Second example: Sometimes it’s better to agree to disagree and move on.

Word 338. Elitist.
Meaning: Someone who believes they are superior to others.
First example: Some people think private schools are elitist.
Second example: He sounded elitist in his speech.

Word 339. Ever since.
Meaning: From a specific time in the past until now.
First example: I have been busy ever since morning.
Second example: She has lived here ever since 2020.

Word 340. Were to.
Meaning: Used to talk about a possible situation.
First example: If I were to start a business, I would sell online.
Second example: If he were to call, tell him I’m busy.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

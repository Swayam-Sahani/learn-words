Vocabulary lesson 14. Words 261 to 280.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 261. Devoted life.
Meaning: Dedicated life to something.
First example: He devoted his life to science.
Second example: She devoted her life to helping others.

Word 262. Hyperactive.
Meaning: Extremely energetic and restless.
First example: The kids were hyperactive after eating sugar.
Second example: He was a hyperactive child.

Word 263. Pursuit.
Meaning: The act of trying to achieve something.
First example: Happiness is a common pursuit in life.
Second example: Education is a noble pursuit.

Word 264. Intellectual pursuit.
Meaning: Activities that involve thinking and learning.
First example: Reading philosophy is an intellectual pursuit.
Second example: Research is an intellectual pursuit.

Word 265. Rich ideas.
Meaning: Creative and valuable ideas.
First example: The discussion produced many rich ideas.
Second example: Entrepreneurs often share rich ideas during brainstorming.

Word 266. Complicate things.
Meaning: Make something more difficult or confusing.
First example: Don’t complicate things; keep it simple.
Second example: Adding too many steps will complicate things.

Word 267. Boil it down.
Meaning: Explain something in a very simple way.
First example: Let me boil it down for you.
Second example: If you boil it down, the problem is about money.

Word 268. Boils down.
Meaning: The main reason or essence of something.
First example: It all boils down to discipline.
Second example: Success boils down to consistency.

Word 269. Anthropology.
Meaning: Study of humans, cultures, and societies.
First example: She studied anthropology in college.
Second example: Anthropology helps us understand human history.

Word 270. Thanks for having me.
Meaning: Polite way to thank someone for inviting you.
First example: Thanks for having me on your podcast.
Second example: Thanks for having me over for dinner.

Word 271. Playful grin.
Meaning: A fun, teasing smile.
First example: He gave a playful grin before joking.
Second example: She had a playful grin on her face.

Word 272. Cherish.
Meaning: To value something deeply.
First example: I cherish the time spent with family.
Second example: She cherishes old memories.

Word 273. Lighthearted moment.
Meaning: A fun, relaxed, and cheerful moment.
First example: The joke created a lighthearted moment.
Second example: We shared a lighthearted moment during lunch.

Word 274. Mug up.
Meaning: Memorize something quickly without deep understanding.
First example: I had to mug up formulas before the exam.
Second example: Students sometimes mug up answers.

Word 275. Epitome.
Meaning: Perfect example of something.
First example: She is the epitome of kindness.
Second example: That car is the epitome of luxury.

Word 276. Exert.
Meaning: Use effort or influence.
First example: You must exert effort to succeed.
Second example: He exerted pressure to finish the work.

Word 277. Swamp.
Meaning: Wet land or to overwhelm with too much.
First example: The area is surrounded by swamps.
Second example: I was swamped with work yesterday.

Word 278. Scarcity.
Meaning: Shortage of something.
First example: Water scarcity is a big issue.
Second example: Scarcity increases the value of resources.

Word 279. Transport.
Meaning: Move people or goods from one place to another.
First example: Trucks transport goods across cities.
Second example: Public transport helps reduce traffic.

Word 280. Dreadful.
Meaning: Very bad or unpleasant.
First example: The weather was dreadful today.
Second example: He had a dreadful headache.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

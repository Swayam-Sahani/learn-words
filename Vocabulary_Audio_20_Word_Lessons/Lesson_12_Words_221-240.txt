Vocabulary lesson 12. Words 221 to 240.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 221. I’m down for it.
Meaning: I’m interested / I agree to do it.
First example: If you want to watch a movie, I’m down for it.
Second example: They’re planning a trip and I’m down for it.

Word 222. Remarkable.
Meaning: Very impressive or unusual.
First example: She did a remarkable job on the project.
Second example: His recovery was remarkable.

Word 223. Getting ahead of myself.
Meaning: Talking or thinking too far in advance.
First example: I might be getting ahead of myself, but this idea could work.
Second example: Let’s not get ahead of ourselves before the results.

Word 224. 1 minute or so.
Meaning: About one minute.
First example: Give me a minute or so, I’ll finish this.
Second example: Wait here for a minute or so.

Word 225. That sucks.
Meaning: Informal way to say something is bad or disappointing.
First example: You lost your phone? That sucks.
Second example: The trip got cancelled? That really sucks.

Word 226. Delivered keynotes.
Meaning: Gave an important speech at an event.
First example: She delivered a keynote at the conference.
Second example: The CEO delivered the keynote speech yesterday.

Word 227. Things go south.
Meaning: When a situation becomes bad.
First example: Everything was fine until things went south.
Second example: The deal went south after the disagreement.

Word 228. Nuance.
Meaning: A small but important difference in meaning.
First example: Language has many nuances.
Second example: Good leaders understand the nuance of situations.

Word 229. Start to lose it.
Meaning: Begin to lose control emotionally or mentally.
First example: He started to lose it under pressure.
Second example: I almost started to lose it during the stressful meeting.

Word 230. Soaked.
Meaning: Completely wet.
First example: I got soaked in the rain.
Second example: My clothes were soaked after the storm.

Word 231. Sledgehammer.
Meaning: A very heavy hammer used to break things.
First example: They used a sledgehammer to break the wall.
Second example: The workers smashed the concrete with a sledgehammer.

Word 232. Start to.
Meaning: Begin doing something.
First example: I start to understand the concept now.
Second example: It started to rain suddenly.

Word 233. Fidgeting.
Meaning: Making small nervous movements.
First example: He kept fidgeting during the interview.
Second example: She was fidgeting with her pen while thinking.

Word 234. Piss you off.
Meaning: (Informal) To make someone very angry.
First example: It really pisses me off when people lie.
Second example: His rude behavior pissed everyone off.

Word 235. Take a dump.
Meaning: (Informal) To go to the toilet to pass stool.
First example: I’ll be back in a minute, I need to take a dump.
Second example: The dog went outside to take a dump.

Word 236. Chronic stress.
Meaning: Long-term continuous stress.
First example: Chronic stress can affect your health.
Second example: Many professionals suffer from chronic stress.

Word 237. Narcissist.
Meaning: A person who is overly self-centered and loves admiration.
First example: He behaves like a narcissist who only cares about himself.
Second example: Narcissists rarely accept criticism.

Word 238. Hardwired.
Meaning: Something natural or built into us.
First example: Humans are hardwired to seek social connection.
Second example: Fear of danger is hardwired in our brains.

Word 239. Proximity.
Meaning: Nearness or closeness.
First example: The restaurant’s proximity to the office is convenient.
Second example: Living in close proximity helps neighbors bond.

Word 240. Dorm (Dormitory).
Meaning: A building where students live.
First example: He lives in the college dorm.
Second example: We had many late-night talks in the dorm.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

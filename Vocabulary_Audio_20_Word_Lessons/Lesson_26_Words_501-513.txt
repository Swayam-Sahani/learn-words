Vocabulary lesson 26. Words 501 to 513.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 501. detriment.
Meaning: Harm or damage to something.
First example: Don't work too hard to the detriment of your health.
Second example: Cutting costs shouldn't be done to the detriment of quality.
Third example: His bad habits were to the detriment of his future.

Word 502. essentially.
Meaning: Basically; at the most important level.
First example: Essentially, I'm trying to improve my English.
Second example: Essentially, our goal is to increase organic traffic.
Third example: Essentially, everyone wants to be happy.

Word 503. adequacy.
Meaning: The quality of being sufficient or good enough.
First example: I'm not sure about the adequacy of this solution.
Second example: We need to check the adequacy of our resources before starting.
Third example: The adequacy of the food and water was questioned.

Word 504. peculiar.
Meaning: Strange, unusual or different.
First example: There's something peculiar about him.
Second example: We noticed a peculiar pattern in the website traffic.
Third example: I heard a peculiar noise coming from the kitchen.

Word 505. ambiguous.
Meaning: Unclear; having more than one possible meaning.
First example: Your answer is a little ambiguous.
Second example: The client's instructions were ambiguous.
Third example: His message was ambiguous, so I didn't know what he meant.

Word 506. pretentious.
Meaning: Trying to appear more important, intelligent or sophisticated than you really are.
First example: Don't be so pretentious.
Second example: The website sounds too pretentious instead of being customer-friendly.
Third example: He uses complicated words just to sound intelligent. It's pretentious.

Word 507. pose no threat.
Meaning: Not be dangerous or harmful.
First example: Don't worry, that dog poses no threat.
Second example: This competitor poses no threat to our business right now.
Third example: The new technology poses no threat to traditional jobs in this situation.

Word 508. cast vote.
Meaning: To officially vote for someone or something.
First example: I went to cast my vote yesterday.
Second example: Every employee will get an opportunity to cast a vote.
Third example: Citizens gathered to cast their votes in the election.

Word 509. off spring.
Meaning: Children or young ones of a person or animal.
First example: They have three offspring.
Second example: The study examined the effects of pollution on animal offspring.
Third example: The bird was protecting its offspring.

Word 510. mondane.
Meaning: Ordinary, boring or repetitive.
First example: I'm tired of doing the same mundane tasks every day.
Second example: Automation can help us avoid mundane office work.
Third example: He wanted to escape his mundane routine.

Word 511. binge watching.
Meaning: Watching many episodes of a show continuously.
First example: I spent the whole weekend binge-watching a series.
Second example: Binge-watching can affect your productivity if you don't manage your time.
Third example: We started watching one episode and ended up binge-watching the entire season.

Word 512. Subjective.
Meaning: Based on personal feelings or opinions.
First example: Beauty is subjective.
Second example: Design preferences are subjective and vary from client to client.
Third example: His review of the movie was subjective.

Word 513. Objective.
Meaning: Based on facts; also means a goal or target.
First example: Let's look at the situation objectively.
Second example: Our main objective is to increase website conversions.
Third example: My objective is to become financially independent.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

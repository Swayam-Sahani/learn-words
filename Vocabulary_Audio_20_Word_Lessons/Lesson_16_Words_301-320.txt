Vocabulary lesson 16. Words 301 to 320.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 301. Back to square one.
Meaning: Starting again from the beginning.
First example: The plan failed, so we are back to square one.
Second example: After losing data, we were back to square one.

Word 302. In return.
Meaning: As a response or exchange.
First example: I helped him, and he supported me in return.
Second example: She gave me a gift, so I invited her in return.

Word 303. Lucrative.
Meaning: Producing a lot of profit.
First example: E-commerce can be a lucrative business.
Second example: He found a lucrative job opportunity abroad.

Word 304. Obscure.
Meaning: Not well known or unclear.
First example: He prefers obscure music bands.
Second example: The meaning of the sentence was obscure.

Word 305. Connoisseur.
Meaning: An expert who appreciates fine things.
First example: He is a connoisseur of fine art.
Second example: She is a connoisseur of good food.

Word 306. Leisurely.
Meaning: Done in a relaxed and unhurried way.
First example: We took a leisurely walk in the evening.
Second example: He enjoys a leisurely breakfast on Sundays.

Word 307. Negligence.
Meaning: Lack of proper care or attention.
First example: The accident happened due to negligence.
Second example: Negligence in work can cause serious problems.

Word 308. Perched.
Meaning: Sitting or resting on something (usually at a height).
First example: The bird was perched on the tree branch.
Second example: He was perched on the edge of the chair.

Word 309. Cheeky maina.
Meaning: A playful or slightly naughty bird (used informally for someone mischievous).
First example: That cheeky maina keeps stealing food from the balcony.
Second example: He behaves like a cheeky maina, always teasing others.

Word 310. Window sill (correct spelling).
Meaning: The flat ledge at the bottom of a window.
First example: She kept a flower pot on the window sill.
Second example: The cat was sitting on the window sill.

Word 311. Snap out of it.
Meaning: To quickly stop feeling sad, upset, or distracted.
First example: You need to snap out of it and focus on work.
Second example: He told himself to snap out of it after the failure.

Word 312. Go barracks (likely meant “go to barracks”).
Meaning: To go back to living quarters (used in military context).
First example: The soldiers were ordered to go to barracks.
Second example: After training, they returned to the barracks.

Word 313. Whatsoever.
Meaning: At all (used for emphasis in negative sentences).
First example: I have no interest whatsoever in that topic.
Second example: There was no help whatsoever from anyone.

Word 314. Lethargic.
Meaning: Feeling tired, slow, and lacking energy.
First example: I feel lethargic after eating too much.
Second example: He was lethargic due to lack of sleep.

Word 315. Self-esteem.
Meaning: Confidence in one’s own worth or abilities.
First example: Good achievements boost self-esteem.
Second example: Failure should not affect your self-esteem.

Word 316. Stimulate.
Meaning: To encourage or increase activity or interest.
First example: This book will stimulate your thinking.
Second example: Exercise helps stimulate blood flow.

Word 317. Confiscated.
Meaning: Taken away officially (often by authority).
First example: The teacher confiscated his phone in class.
Second example: Illegal goods were confiscated by the police.

Word 318. Slack off.
Meaning: To avoid work or do something lazily.
First example: Don’t slack off during important tasks.
Second example: He started to slack off after getting comfortable in his job.

Word 319. Perseverance.
Meaning: Continued effort despite difficulties.
First example: Success comes with perseverance.
Second example: Her perseverance helped her achieve her goals.

Word 320. Ever since.
Meaning: From a past time until now.
First example: I’ve been busy ever since I started this job.
Second example: He has been happy ever since he moved here.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

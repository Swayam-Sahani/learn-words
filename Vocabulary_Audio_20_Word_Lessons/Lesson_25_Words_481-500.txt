Vocabulary lesson 25. Words 481 to 500.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 481. correlation and causation.
Meaning: Correlation means two things are related; causation means one causes the other.
First example: Just because two things happen together doesn't mean one causes the other.
Second example: We found a correlation between website traffic and sales, but that doesn't prove causation.
Third example: There is a difference between correlation and causation.

Word 482. walked up to me.
Meaning: Approached me by walking.
First example: A stranger walked up to me and asked for directions.
Second example: A client walked up to me after the presentation.
Third example: He walked up to me and started asking questions.

Word 483. by far.
Meaning: Used to emphasize that something is much more than others.
First example: This is by far my favourite movie.
Second example: This is by far the best-performing campaign.
Third example: He is by far the most talented player on the team.

Word 484. by all means.
Meaning: Certainly; go ahead.
First example: By all means, take my seat.
Second example: By all means, share your feedback.
Third example: If you want to try it, by all means, go ahead.

Word 485. marginal growth.
Meaning: Very small or slight growth.
First example: My savings showed marginal growth this month.
Second example: The company experienced only marginal growth in revenue.
Third example: The business is growing, but the growth is marginal.

Word 486. error of margin.
Meaning: The possible amount of error in a measurement or estimate.
First example: There might be a small margin of error in this calculation.
Second example: The survey has a margin of error of 3%.
Third example: We should consider the margin of error before making a decision.

Word 487. cadence.
Meaning: Rhythm, flow or regular pattern.
First example: I like the cadence of the way he speaks.
Second example: We need a consistent content publishing cadence.
Third example: The speaker's cadence made the story interesting.

Word 488. pre-empted.
Meaning: Took action before something happened to prevent or anticipate it.
First example: He pre-empted my question by answering it first.
Second example: We pre-empted the client's concerns by preparing a detailed report.
Third example: The police pre-empted the attack before it happened.

Word 489. ethos.
Meaning: The beliefs, values and principles of a person or organization.
First example: Honesty is part of my personal ethos.
Second example: Customer satisfaction is central to our company's ethos.
Third example: The school's ethos encourages discipline and creativity.

Word 490. subjective.
Meaning: Based on personal feelings or opinions.
First example: Beauty is subjective.
Second example: Design preferences are subjective, so we should consider the target audience.
Third example: His opinion about the movie is completely subjective.

Word 491. intricacies.
Meaning: Small details or complicated parts of something.
First example: I don't understand all the intricacies of this game.
Second example: We need to understand the intricacies of SEO before building the strategy.
Third example: He explained the intricacies of the legal system.

Word 492. roots for them.
Meaning: Supports or wants someone to succeed.
First example: I always root for my favourite team.
Second example: Our entire team is rooting for you.
Third example: Even though they are competitors, I still root for them.

Word 493. virtue signaling.
Meaning: Showing off moral values to gain public approval rather than genuinely acting on them.
First example: Sometimes people post things online just for virtue signalling.
Second example: A company shouldn't rely on virtue signalling instead of taking real action.
Third example: He was accused of virtue signalling rather than genuinely helping people.

Word 494. too clichéd.
Meaning: Too common, predictable or unoriginal.
First example: That romantic dialogue is too clichéd.
Second example: This marketing campaign feels too clichéd.
Third example: The movie had a predictable and too-clichéd ending.

Word 495. come up to me.
Meaning: Approach me; move towards me.
First example: You can come up to me if you need help.
Second example: The client came up to me with a new idea.
Third example: A little boy came up to me and asked my name.

Word 496. lucrative.
Meaning: Producing a lot of money; profitable.
First example: Freelancing can be a lucrative career.
Second example: SEO services can become a lucrative business with the right clients.
Third example: He left his job for a more lucrative opportunity.

Word 497. ignorant.
Meaning: Lacking knowledge or awareness.
First example: I'm ignorant about this subject, so please explain it to me.
Second example: We shouldn't make decisions based on ignorant assumptions.
Third example: He remained ignorant of what was happening around him.

Word 498. triage.
Meaning: Prioritize problems according to urgency and importance.
First example: I need to triage my tasks before starting work.
Second example: The support team uses triage to handle urgent client issues first.
Third example: Doctors performed triage to treat the most critical patients first.

Word 499. receded.
Meaning: Moved backwards, decreased or became less noticeable.
First example: The water gradually receded after the flood.
Second example: Our website traffic receded after the campaign ended.
Third example: His hairline has receded over the years.

Word 500. comprehend.
Meaning: To fully understand something.
First example: I couldn't comprehend what he was saying.
Second example: It's important to comprehend the client's requirements before starting the project.
Third example: It took me years to comprehend the importance of time.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

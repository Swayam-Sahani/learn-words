Vocabulary lesson 24. Words 461 to 480.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 461. feeling sluggish.
Meaning: Feeling slow, tired or low on energy.
First example: I'm feeling sluggish today.
Second example: I feel sluggish after sitting at my desk all day.
Third example: I usually feel sluggish after eating a heavy meal.

Word 462. year of slog.
Meaning: A year of hard, tiring and continuous work.
First example: It's been a year of slog, but I'm finally seeing results.
Second example: After a year of slog, we finally completed the project.
Third example: It took a whole year of slog to build the business.

Word 463. thats not the case.
Meaning: That's not true; the situation is different.
First example: You think I'm lazy, but that's not the case.
Second example: People think SEO is dead, but that's not the case.
Third example: It may look easy, but that's not the case.

Word 464. pull these things off.
Meaning: Successfully accomplish these tasks.
First example: I don't know how you pull these things off.
Second example: We need a skilled team to pull these things off.
Third example: If we work together, we can pull these things off.

Word 465. cultivate.
Meaning: Develop or improve something gradually.
First example: You should cultivate good habits.
Second example: We need to cultivate stronger relationships with clients.
Third example: Reading helps cultivate critical thinking.

Word 466. inculcate.
Meaning: Instil an idea, value or habit through teaching.
First example: Parents should inculcate good values in their children.
Second example: Companies should inculcate a culture of accountability.
Third example: Schools should inculcate discipline among students.

Word 467. put in the spot.
Meaning: Make someone answer or act under pressure.
First example: Don't put me on the spot like that!
Second example: The manager put me on the spot during the meeting.
Third example: He put her on the spot by asking a personal question.

Word 468. resonate.
Meaning: Connect emotionally or feel meaningful.
First example: That song really resonates with me.
Second example: Your business idea resonates with our target audience.
Third example: His story resonated with millions of people.

Word 469. patronizing.
Meaning: Treating someone as if they are less intelligent or important.
First example: Stop talking to me in that patronizing tone.
Second example: The manager's patronizing attitude upset the employees.
Third example: I don't like people who act patronizing towards others.

Word 470. change things around.
Meaning: Make changes or rearrange things.
First example: Let's change things around a little.
Second example: We need to change things around in our marketing strategy.
Third example: Moving to another city completely changed things around for me.

Word 471. let alone.
Meaning: Used to emphasize that something is even less likely.
First example: I can't afford a bike, let alone a car.
Second example: We can't finish one project, let alone five.
Third example: He can't manage his own expenses, let alone support a family.

Word 472. speculation.
Meaning: Guessing without having confirmed information.
First example: That's just speculation; we don't know what happened.
Second example: The market is full of speculation right now.
Third example: There was a lot of speculation about his resignation.

Word 473. haul.
Meaning: A large amount of something collected, bought or carried.
First example: Look at my shopping haul!
Second example: We received a huge haul of new products.
Third example: The police discovered a large haul of stolen goods.

Word 474. pipe dream.
Meaning: An unrealistic dream or hope.
First example: Becoming rich overnight is just a pipe dream.
Second example: Without proper funding, this business plan could remain a pipe dream.
Third example: He thought travelling the world was a pipe dream until he saved enough money.

Word 475. despair.
Meaning: Complete loss of hope.
First example: Don't lose hope and fall into despair.
Second example: The business owner was in despair after losing his biggest client.
Third example: He looked at the situation with complete despair.

Word 476. immobilize.
Meaning: Make someone or something unable to move.
First example: The injury immobilized my leg.
Second example: The technical issue immobilized our entire system.
Third example: The heavy snowfall immobilized traffic across the city.

Word 477. parables.
Meaning: Short stories that teach moral or spiritual lessons.
First example: My grandmother used to tell me interesting parables.
Second example: The speaker used parables to explain leadership lessons.
Third example: The book contains several parables about honesty and kindness.

Word 478. spectrum.
Meaning: A wide range of things or opinions.
First example: People have a whole spectrum of opinions.
Second example: We offer services across a wide spectrum of industries.
Third example: Emotions exist on a broad spectrum.

Word 479. its relative.
Meaning: It depends on the situation or perspective.
First example: Being rich is relative; it means different things to different people.
Second example: Success is relative to your business goals.
Third example: What's expensive to one person might be affordable to another.

Word 480. mockery.
Meaning: Making fun of someone or something.
First example: Don't make a mockery of someone's feelings.
Second example: The poor presentation made a mockery of our preparation.
Third example: His dream became a subject of mockery among his friends.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

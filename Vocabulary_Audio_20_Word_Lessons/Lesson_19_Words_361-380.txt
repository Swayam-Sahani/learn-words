Vocabulary lesson 19. Words 361 to 380.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 361. Convicted.
Meaning: Found guilty of a crime by a court.
First example: The thief was convicted last year.
Second example: He was convicted for fraud.

Word 362. Morality.
Meaning: Principles about right and wrong behavior.
First example: Parents teach children morality.
Second example: Business decisions should consider morality.

Word 363. Retarded (correct spelling).
Meaning: Delayed or slowed development (medical/technical term; can be offensive in casual use).
First example: The project progress was retarded due to lack of funds.
Second example: Growth was retarded by poor planning.

Word 364. Condemn.
Meaning: To strongly criticize or express disapproval.
First example: Leaders condemned the violent attack.
Second example: We should condemn corruption.

Word 365. Dignity.
Meaning: A sense of self-respect and honor.
First example: Everyone deserves to live with dignity.
Second example: She handled the situation with dignity.

Word 366. Fascist (correct spelling).
Meaning: Someone who supports strict authoritarian control.
First example: The group was accused of having fascist ideas.
Second example: People protested against fascist policies.

Word 367. Testify.
Meaning: To give evidence or speak formally about something.
First example: He will testify in court tomorrow.
Second example: The witness testified about the incident.

Word 368. Regime change.
Meaning: Replacing one government or leadership with another.
First example: The country experienced regime change after the election.
Second example: Some groups demanded regime change.

Word 369. An act of.
Meaning: Something done that shows a particular quality.
First example: Helping the poor is an act of kindness.
Second example: Donating blood is an act of courage.

Word 370. For God's sake.
Meaning: Expression of frustration or urgency.
First example: For God's sake, hurry up!
Second example: For God's sake, listen carefully.

Word 371. Despise.
Meaning: To strongly dislike or hate.
First example: I despise dishonesty.
Second example: She despises rude behavior.

Word 372. Bunch of.
Meaning: A group of people or things.
First example: A bunch of students entered the room.
Second example: I bought a bunch of bananas.

Word 373. Radical.
Meaning: Very extreme or favoring big changes.
First example: He proposed radical changes in the company.
Second example: Some ideas sound too radical.

Word 374. That's absurd.
Meaning: That is completely unreasonable or ridiculous.
First example: Charging that much money is absurd.
Second example: That's absurd — it makes no sense.

Word 375. Consecutive.
Meaning: Following one after another without a break.
First example: He worked for five consecutive days.
Second example: The team won three consecutive matches.

Word 376. Prejudice.
Meaning: Unfair opinion about someone without facts.
First example: We should avoid prejudice.
Second example: The decision was based on prejudice.

Word 377. Frightened.
Meaning: Feeling scared.
First example: The child was frightened by the noise.
Second example: I felt frightened during the storm.

Word 378. Not following you.
Meaning: Not understanding what someone is saying.
First example: Sorry, I'm not following you.
Second example: Can you explain again? I'm not following you.

Word 379. Won't budge.
Meaning: Refuses to change opinion or move.
First example: He won't budge from his decision.
Second example: The manager won't budge on the price.

Word 380. Decisive.
Meaning: Able to make decisions quickly and confidently.
First example: A leader must be decisive.
Second example: Her decisive action solved the problem.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 5. Words 81 to 100.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 81. Cynically.
Meaning: In a distrustful or negative way.
First example: He cynically laughed at the proposal.
Second example: She cynically questioned their intentions.

Word 82. Stimulated.
Meaning: Encouraged activity or interest.
First example: The discussion stimulated new ideas.
Second example: The book stimulated my curiosity.

Word 83. Rumination.
Meaning: Deep or repetitive thinking about something.
First example: Too much rumination can cause stress.
Second example: He spent the evening in quiet rumination.

Word 84. Let me vent.
Meaning: Let me express my frustration.
First example: I had a bad day; let me vent for a minute.
Second example: Sometimes people just need to vent.

Word 85. Fatigue.
Meaning: Extreme tiredness.
First example: Long work hours cause fatigue.
Second example: He felt mental fatigue after studying all day.

Word 86. Rundown.
Meaning: A brief explanation or summary.
First example: Give me a quick rundown of the meeting.
Second example: He gave a rundown of the project status.

Word 87. Optimist.
Meaning: Someone who expects good outcomes.
First example: She is an optimist about the future.
Second example: An optimist sees opportunities everywhere.

Word 88. Exhausted.
Meaning: Extremely tired.
First example: I was exhausted after the workout.
Second example: He felt exhausted after working late.

Word 89. Driven by.
Meaning: Motivated or powered by something.
First example: He is driven by ambition.
Second example: Innovation is driven by curiosity.

Word 90. Get to choose.
Meaning: Have the opportunity to select.
First example: You get to choose your career path.
Second example: Customers get to choose different options.

Word 91. Paradox.
Meaning: A situation that seems contradictory but may be true.
First example: It’s a paradox that success can bring stress.
Second example: The idea sounds like a paradox.

Word 92. Piece of advice.
Meaning: A helpful suggestion.
First example: Let me give you a piece of advice.
Second example: My father gave me a valuable piece of advice.

Word 93. Simultaneously.
Meaning: At the same time.
First example: He managed two projects simultaneously.
Second example: The lights turned on simultaneously.

Word 94. Affirmation machine.
Meaning: Something that constantly gives praise or approval.
First example: Social media sometimes becomes an affirmation machine.
Second example: People often look for an affirmation machine online.

Word 95. Affirmations.
Meaning: Positive statements to encourage confidence.
First example: Daily affirmations can improve mindset.
Second example: She starts her morning with affirmations.

Word 96. Moderate.
Meaning: Not extreme; balanced.
First example: A moderate approach works best.
Second example: Exercise in moderate amounts is healthy.

Word 97. Seek validation.
Meaning: Look for approval from others.
First example: Many people seek validation on social media.
Second example: You shouldn't always seek validation from others.

Word 98. Grit.
Meaning: Courage and determination to keep going.
First example: Success often requires grit.
Second example: She achieved her goals through pure grit.

Word 99. Confrontation.
Meaning: A direct disagreement or conflict.
First example: I try to avoid unnecessary confrontation.
Second example: The meeting turned into a heated confrontation.

Word 100. Part of the equation.
Meaning: One factor in a situation.
First example: Hard work is only part of the equation.
Second example: Money is part of the equation, but not everything.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 15. Words 281 to 300.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 281. Dumpster.
Meaning: Large container for garbage.
First example: They threw the waste in the dumpster.
Second example: The alley had a big dumpster.

Word 282. Battered.
Meaning: Damaged by repeated hits.
First example: The old car looked battered.
Second example: The boat was battered by the storm.

Word 283. Prune.
Meaning: Cut parts of a plant to help it grow better.
First example: Farmers prune trees regularly.
Second example: You should prune plants in winter.

Word 284. Rigged.
Meaning: Manipulated unfairly.
First example: Some people think the game was rigged.
Second example: The system seemed rigged against small players.

Word 285. Reactive.
Meaning: Responding to events after they happen.
First example: He tends to be reactive instead of proactive.
Second example: Reactive decisions often cause problems.

Word 286. Proactive.
Meaning: Taking action in advance instead of reacting later.
First example: Good leaders are proactive and solve problems early.
Second example: Try to be proactive about your health.

Word 287. Vicious circle.
Meaning: A problem where one bad thing leads to another and keeps repeating.
First example: Debt and stress can create a vicious circle.
Second example: Lack of sleep and stress often become a vicious circle.

Word 288. Spent last decade.
Meaning: Used the past ten years doing something.
First example: I spent the last decade building my business.
Second example: She spent the last decade studying climate change.

Word 289. Right there and then.
Meaning: Immediately at that moment.
First example: I decided right there and then to quit my job.
Second example: He apologized right there and then.

Word 290. You name it, I’ve done it.
Meaning: I have done many different things.
First example: I’ve tried many jobs—sales, marketing, design—you name it, I’ve done it.
Second example: In business, you name it, I’ve done it.

Word 291. Cut him loose.
Meaning: Let someone go / stop supporting them.
First example: The company cut him loose after repeated mistakes.
Second example: If he keeps breaking rules, they may cut him loose.

Word 292. Far out.
Meaning: (Informal) Very surprising, amazing, or unbelievable.
First example: That idea is far out but interesting.
Second example: His story sounded far out to me.

Word 293. Anticipation.
Meaning: A feeling of excitement about something that will happen.
First example: I’m waiting in anticipation for the results.
Second example: There was a lot of anticipation before the event started.

Word 294. Distinctly changed.
Meaning: Clearly or noticeably different.
First example: His behavior has distinctly changed after the promotion.
Second example: The market trend has distinctly changed this year.

Word 295. Stop whining.
Meaning: Stop complaining in an annoying way.
First example: Stop whining and focus on solving the problem.
Second example: He told his friend to stop whining about small issues.

Word 296. Step forward.
Meaning: To take initiative or move ahead.
First example: She decided to step forward and lead the team.
Second example: Someone needs to step forward in this situation.

Word 297. Stranded.
Meaning: Left without help in a difficult place.
First example: We were stranded on the highway due to a breakdown.
Second example: The travelers were stranded because of bad weather.

Word 298. Pertains.
Meaning: Relates to or is about something.
First example: This rule pertains to all employees.
Second example: The discussion pertains to business growth.

Word 299. Snob.
Meaning: A person who thinks they are superior to others.
First example: Don’t act like a snob in front of others.
Second example: He is a snob when it comes to branded clothes.

Word 300. Condescending.
Meaning: Talking in a way that shows you think you are better than others.
First example: His condescending tone annoyed everyone.
Second example: Don’t be condescending while giving advice.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 21. Words 401 to 420.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 401. captive.
Meaning: Someone held prisoner or unable to escape.
First example: The soldiers kept him captive.
Second example: The audience was captive to his presentation.
Third example: The animals were held captive for years.

Word 402. stigma.
Meaning: A negative social judgment or label.
First example: There's still a stigma around failure.
Second example: We should remove the stigma associated with mental health.
Third example: Losing a job shouldn't carry a social stigma.

Word 403. decimated.
Meaning: Destroyed or severely reduced.
First example: The storm decimated the village.
Second example: Poor management decimated the company's profits.
Third example: The disease decimated the population.

Word 404. tattered.
Meaning: Old, torn or damaged.
First example: He's wearing a tattered shirt.
Second example: The company's reputation was left tattered after the incident.
Third example: An old man was carrying a tattered bag.

Word 405. intercept.
Meaning: To stop or catch something before it reaches its destination.
First example: The police intercepted the vehicle.
Second example: We intercepted the issue before it affected the project.
Third example: The goalkeeper intercepted the ball.

Word 406. dellict.
Meaning: Abandoned, neglected or in very poor condition.
First example: There's a derelict building near my house.
Second example: The company purchased a derelict property.
Third example: We walked past a derelict factory.

Word 407. endeavors.
Meaning: Efforts, attempts or activities.
First example: I wish you success in your future endeavors.
Second example: We appreciate your efforts and professional endeavors.
Third example: Despite several failures, he continued his endeavors.

Word 408. bear minimum.
Meaning: The least amount required.
First example: I'm just doing the bare minimum today.
Second example: He only completed the bare minimum of his responsibilities.
Third example: Don't settle for the bare minimum in life.

Word 409. bear with me.
Meaning: Be patient with me.
First example: Bear with me for a minute.
Second example: Please bear with me while I check the details.
Third example: Bear with me; I'll explain everything shortly.

Word 410. rip off.
Meaning: Cheat someone by charging too much or selling something unfairly.
First example: This shop completely ripped me off.
Second example: The agency charged us too much. It was a rip-off.
Third example: I paid ₹2,000 for this? What a rip-off!

Word 411. yet again.
Meaning: Once more; again.
First example: You're late yet again!
Second example: The client has requested changes yet again.
Third example: Yet again, things didn't go as planned.

Word 412. obliterate.
Meaning: Completely destroy or wipe out.
First example: The explosion obliterated the building.
Second example: A strong competitor could obliterate a weak business.
Third example: The flood obliterated everything in its path.

Word 413. dime a dozen.
Meaning: Very common; not special or unique.
First example: These kinds of excuses are a dime a dozen.
Second example: Basic websites are a dime a dozen nowadays.
Third example: Fake promises are a dime a dozen.

Word 414. satire.
Meaning: Humour used to criticize society, people or politics.
First example: I enjoy watching political satire.
Second example: The advertisement uses satire to highlight bad business practices.
Third example: The movie is a satire on modern society.

Word 415. stash.
Meaning: To hide or store something for later.
First example: I have a stash of chocolates in my room.
Second example: Keep a stash of backup files just in case.
Third example: He had stashed some money under his bed.

Word 416. hold onto this.
Meaning: Keep this; don't let it go.
First example: Hold onto this bag for a minute.
Second example: Hold onto this document until the project is finished.
Third example: You should hold onto your dreams.

Word 417. infested.
Meaning: Filled with unwanted insects, animals or harmful things.
First example: My kitchen is infested with ants.
Second example: The old office was infested with rats.
Third example: The area was infested with mosquitoes.

Word 418. dress up.
Meaning: Wear special or formal clothes.
First example: We should dress up for the party.
Second example: You don't need to dress up for this meeting.
Third example: She loves dressing up for special occasions.

Word 419. tussle.
Meaning: A physical struggle or argument.
First example: They got into a tussle over a parking space.
Second example: The two companies are in a tussle over the contract.
Third example: He had a little tussle with his brother.

Word 420. get hang of it.
Meaning: Learn how to do something.
First example: I'm slowly getting the hang of driving.
Second example: It took me a week to get the hang of this software.
Third example: Don't worry, you'll get the hang of it eventually.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

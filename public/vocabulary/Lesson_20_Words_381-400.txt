Vocabulary lesson 20. Words 381 to 400.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 381. Whatsoever.
Meaning: At all; in any way.
First example: I have no doubt whatsoever.
Second example: There was no problem whatsoever.

Word 382. Yapping.
Meaning: Talking a lot in an annoying way.
First example: He kept yapping during the meeting.
Second example: Stop yapping and focus on work.

Word 383. Yet again.
Meaning: Once more; another time.
First example: He made the same mistake yet again.
Second example: The system failed yet again.

Word 384. Abruptly.
Meaning: Suddenly and unexpectedly.
First example: The meeting ended abruptly.
Second example: He left the room abruptly.

Word 385. Weaselly (likely intended; meaning tricky or dishonest).
Meaning: Acting in a sneaky or dishonest way.
First example: He gave a weaselly excuse.
Second example: That was a weaselly move.

Word 386. Indisputable.
Meaning: Impossible to deny or question.
First example: The evidence is indisputable.
Second example: Her success is indisputable.

Word 387. Down the road.
Meaning: In the future; later on.
First example: We may expand the business down the road.
Second example: This decision will help us down the road.

Word 388. Analogy.
Meaning: A comparison used to explain something.
First example: The teacher used an analogy to explain the concept.
Second example: He gave an analogy between business and sports.

Word 389. Hypothetical.
Meaning: Based on imagination or assumption, not real.
First example: Let's discuss a hypothetical situation.
Second example: That was just a hypothetical question.

Word 390. Relentless.
Meaning: Continuing without stopping or giving up.
First example: She made relentless efforts to grow her business.
Second example: The rain was relentless all night.

Word 391. Perpetrators.
Meaning: People who commit a crime or wrongdoing.
First example: The police arrested the perpetrators.
Second example: The perpetrators will face punishment.

Word 392. Firmly denied.
Meaning: Strongly said something is not true.
First example: He firmly denied the allegations.
Second example: The company firmly denied any wrongdoing.

Word 393. Reluctant.
Meaning: Not willing or hesitant to do something.
First example: I was reluctant to change my job.
Second example: She felt reluctant to speak in public.

Word 394. Puny.
Meaning: Very small or weak; insignificant.
First example: The puppy looked puny and weak.
Second example: That amount of money is puny compared to the cost.

Word 395. struck me as.
Meaning: Gave me an impression of something.
First example: He struck me as a very friendly person.
Second example: The proposal struck me as a good business opportunity.
Third example: Something about that place struck me as unusual.

Word 396. menacing.
Meaning: Threatening, frightening or dangerous.
First example: That dog looks menacing.
Second example: His aggressive tone was quite menacing.
Third example: Dark clouds were gathering in a menacing way.

Word 397. turmoil.
Meaning: A state of confusion, trouble or disturbance.
First example: My mind is in turmoil right now.
Second example: The company is going through financial turmoil.
Third example: The country experienced political turmoil.

Word 398. tend to be.
Meaning: Usually behave or happen in a particular way.
First example: I tend to be quiet around strangers.
Second example: Clients tend to be more interested in affordable solutions.
Third example: People tend to forget small things over time.

Word 399. bit of scoop.
Meaning: A little inside information or secret news.
First example: I've got a bit of scoop for you.
Second example: Do you have a bit of scoop about the new project?
Third example: Let me give you a bit of scoop about what happened yesterday.

Word 400. amid.
Meaning: In the middle of something; surrounded by.
First example: Amid all the noise, I couldn't hear you.
Second example: We launched the project amid several challenges.
Third example: Amid the chaos, he remained calm.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

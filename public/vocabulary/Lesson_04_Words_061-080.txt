Vocabulary lesson 4. Words 61 to 80.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 61. Conceive.
Meaning: To think of or imagine an idea.
First example: It’s hard to conceive such a complex plan.
Second example: She conceived a brilliant business idea.

Word 62. Extremism.
Meaning: Very radical or extreme beliefs.
First example: Society should reject extremism.
Second example: Extremism often creates conflict.

Word 63. Went out of business.
Meaning: A company closed permanently.
First example: Many shops went out of business during the pandemic.
Second example: The restaurant went out of business last year.

Word 64. Democracy, capitalism, communism.
Meaning: Different political/economic systems.
First example: Democracy focuses on people’s voting power.
Second example: Capitalism allows private ownership and free markets. 3. Communism promotes collective ownership.

Word 65. Existential tension.
Meaning: Deep anxiety about meaning or purpose of life.
First example: Many people feel existential tension in uncertain times.
Second example: Philosophy often explores existential tension.

Word 66. Detriment.
Meaning: Harm or damage.
First example: Too much stress is a detriment to health.
Second example: Ignoring feedback can be a detriment to growth.

Word 67. Benefits and detractors.
Meaning: Supporters and critics of something.
First example: Every technology has benefits and detractors.
Second example: The policy has many benefits and detractors.

Word 68. Corollary.
Meaning: A natural result or conclusion.
First example: A corollary of hard work is success.
Second example: A logical corollary follows from this idea.

Word 69. Awful.
Meaning: Very bad or unpleasant.
First example: The food tasted awful.
Second example: I had an awful headache yesterday.

Word 70. Plenty.
Meaning: More than enough.
First example: We have plenty of time.
Second example: There are plenty of opportunities online.

Word 71. Ridiculous.
Meaning: Very silly or unreasonable.
First example: That price is ridiculous.
Second example: His excuse sounded ridiculous.

Word 72. High and mighty.
Meaning: Acting superior or arrogant.
First example: He acts high and mighty around others.
Second example: Don’t behave high and mighty with your team.

Word 73. Reinforce.
Meaning: Strengthen or support something.
First example: Good habits reinforce success.
Second example: The teacher reinforced the idea with examples.

Word 74. Humility.
Meaning: Being humble; not arrogant.
First example: True leaders show humility.
Second example: Success should be balanced with humility.

Word 75. Camaraderie.
Meaning: Friendly trust among people.
First example: There is great camaraderie in our team.
Second example: Sports often build camaraderie.

Word 76. Espresso and core.
Meaning: A strong coffee / “core” means central part.
First example: I start my day with an espresso.
Second example: Discipline is the core of success.

Word 77. Pull over.
Meaning: Stop a vehicle at the side of the road.
First example: The police asked him to pull over.
Second example: Let’s pull over and check the map.

Word 78. Enlisted.
Meaning: Joined the military or a service.
First example: He enlisted in the army at 20.
Second example: Many young people enlist to serve their country.

Word 79. Fraternizing.
Meaning: Socializing in a friendly way (sometimes improperly).
First example: Employees are not allowed to fraternize with clients too much.
Second example: The soldiers were warned about fraternizing.

Word 80. Articulated.
Meaning: Expressed clearly in words.
First example: She articulated her ideas very well.
Second example: The speaker articulated the problem clearly.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

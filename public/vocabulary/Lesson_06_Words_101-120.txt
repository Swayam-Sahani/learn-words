Vocabulary lesson 6. Words 101 to 120.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 101. Conflict avoidance.
Meaning: Trying to avoid disagreements.
First example: His conflict avoidance sometimes causes bigger problems.
Second example: Conflict avoidance is common in many workplaces.

Word 102. Did it with wide eyes open.
Meaning: Did something fully aware of the risks.
First example: I started the business with my eyes wide open.
Second example: She entered the deal with her eyes wide open.

Word 103. Binary construction.
Meaning: Thinking in only two options (yes/no, right/wrong).
First example: The problem isn’t a binary construction.
Second example: Life decisions are rarely binary.

Word 104. Wax and wane.
Meaning: Rise and fall over time.
First example: Motivation tends to wax and wane.
Second example: Interest in trends often waxes and wanes.

Word 105. Applauded.
Meaning: Praised or appreciated.
First example: The audience applauded his speech.
Second example: Her effort was widely applauded.

Word 106. Got you covered.
Meaning: Means someone will take care of it.
First example: Don’t worry, I’ve got you covered.
Second example: If you miss the meeting, I’ll cover the notes for you.

Word 107. Balancing act.
Meaning: Trying to manage multiple things at once.
First example: Work-life balance is a constant balancing act.
Second example: Managing a business is a balancing act.

Word 108. Adversely proportionate (likely “inversely proportionate”).
Meaning: When one increases, the other decreases.
First example: Stress is often inversely proportionate to preparation.
Second example: Price and demand can be inversely proportionate.

Word 109. Goes hand in hand.
Meaning: Two things are closely connected.
First example: Success goes hand in hand with discipline.
Second example: Trust and communication go hand in hand.

Word 110. Flashiest.
Meaning: Most showy or attention-grabbing.
First example: He bought the flashiest car in the showroom.
Second example: The flashiest designs often attract customers.

Word 111. Pay attention.
Meaning: Focus carefully.
First example: Pay attention to the details.
Second example: You should pay attention during meetings.

Word 112. Show up.
Meaning: Arrive or appear.
First example: Success often means just showing up every day.
Second example: He didn’t show up to the meeting.

Word 113. Screw up.
Meaning: Make a mistake.
First example: Everyone screws up sometimes.
Second example: I really screwed up that presentation.

Word 114. Stakes are low (correct phrase).
Meaning: The risk or consequences are small.
First example: Try new ideas when the stakes are low.
Second example: Practice when the stakes are low, perform when they’re high.

Word 115. Eternal.
Meaning: Lasting forever; never ending.
First example: People often search for eternal happiness.
Second example: Their friendship felt almost eternal.

Word 116. Never sought understanding.
Meaning: Never tried to understand something.
First example: He criticized the idea but never sought understanding.
Second example: Many conflicts happen because people never seek understanding.

Word 117. Enduring loneliness.
Meaning: Long-lasting feeling of being alone.
First example: Moving to a new city can cause enduring loneliness.
Second example: Some elderly people suffer from enduring loneliness.

Word 118. Inevitable.
Meaning: Certain to happen; unavoidable.
First example: Change is inevitable in life.
Second example: Mistakes are inevitable when you try new things.

Word 119. Despair.
Meaning: Complete loss of hope.
First example: He felt deep despair after the failure.
Second example: Don’t let temporary problems lead to despair.

Word 120. Something’s off.
Meaning: Something feels wrong or unusual.
First example: I don’t know why, but something’s off today.
Second example: The numbers look strange—something’s off.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

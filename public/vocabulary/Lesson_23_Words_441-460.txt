Vocabulary lesson 23. Words 441 to 460.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 441. couldn't have been a more of a disaster.
Meaning: Something was an absolute disaster.
First example: My first driving lesson couldn't have been more of a disaster.
Second example: The product launch couldn't have been more of a disaster.
Third example: Our holiday couldn't have been more of a disaster.

Word 442. looking forward to.
Meaning: Feeling excited about something in the future.
First example: I'm looking forward to the weekend.
Second example: I'm looking forward to working with your team.
Third example: I'm really looking forward to meeting you.

Word 443. clench your fist.
Meaning: Close your hand tightly.
First example: He clenched his fist in anger.
Second example: Try not to clench your fists when you're nervous.
Third example: She clenched her fists and prepared for the fight.

Word 444. lineage.
Meaning: Family history or ancestral background.
First example: He comes from a royal lineage.
Second example: The company has a long lineage of experienced founders.
Third example: Scientists studied the lineage of the ancient species.

Word 445. pull off.
Meaning: Successfully achieve something difficult.
First example: I can't believe you pulled it off!
Second example: We managed to pull off the project before the deadline.
Third example: Nobody thought he could win, but he pulled it off.

Word 446. got jumpy.
Meaning: Became nervous, anxious or easily startled.
First example: I got jumpy when I heard that noise.
Second example: The client got jumpy when we missed the deadline.
Third example: He got jumpy after watching that horror movie.

Word 447. stand down.
Meaning: Stop fighting, arguing or taking action.
First example: Relax, you can stand down now.
Second example: The manager asked the team to stand down until further instructions.
Third example: The police told the officers to stand down.

Word 448. bye all means.
Meaning: Certainly; go ahead; feel free.
First example: By all means, take another piece of cake.
Second example: By all means, share your suggestions.
Third example: If you want to join us, by all means, come along.

Word 449. now then.
Meaning: Used to get attention or introduce a new topic.
First example: Now then, what should we do today?
Second example: Now then, let's discuss the next steps.
Third example: Now then, where were we?

Word 450. upper hand.
Meaning: An advantage or position of control.
First example: He always wants to have the upper hand in an argument.
Second example: Our competitors currently have the upper hand.
Third example: She gained the upper hand during the negotiation.

Word 451. stigma.
Meaning: A negative social label or judgment.
First example: There's a stigma around failure.
Second example: We need to remove the stigma associated with mental health.
Third example: People shouldn't face stigma because of their background.

Word 452. caveats.
Meaning: Warnings, conditions or limitations.
First example: There is one caveat: you have to return it tomorrow.
Second example: The proposal looks good, but there are a few caveats.
Third example: The deal comes with several caveats.

Word 453. go on.
Meaning: Continue; proceed; tell me more.
First example: Go on, I'm listening.
Second example: Please go on with your presentation.
Third example: He went on talking for another hour.

Word 454. ick.
Meaning: A feeling of disgust or sudden loss of attraction.
First example: That smell gives me the ick.
Second example: Poor communication can give customers the ick.
Third example: I got the ick when he started behaving rudely.

Word 455. ruthlessly.
Meaning: Without mercy or sympathy; very aggressively.
First example: He ruthlessly criticized everyone.
Second example: The company ruthlessly cut unnecessary expenses.
Third example: The king ruled the kingdom ruthlessly.

Word 456. let that sink in.
Meaning: Take time to understand how important something is.
First example: He earns ₹1 lakh a day. Let that sink in.
Second example: We lost 30% of our customers. Let that sink in.
Third example: You only get one life. Let that sink in.

Word 457. I have nothing against you.
Meaning: I don't dislike you or have a personal problem with you.
First example: I have nothing against you; I just disagree with your decision.
Second example: I have nothing against you personally, but I don't support this idea.
Third example: Don't misunderstand me. I have nothing against you.

Word 458. you really had me there.
Meaning: You fooled or surprised me; I believed you for a moment.
First example: I thought you were serious! You really had me there.
Second example: When you said the project was cancelled, you really had me there.
Third example: I thought you had actually won the lottery. You really had me there!

Word 459. sudden turn of events.
Meaning: An unexpected change in a situation.
First example: There was a sudden turn of events, and we had to cancel the trip.
Second example: A sudden turn of events changed the entire business strategy.
Third example: Everything was going well until a sudden turn of events changed our lives.

Word 460. for that matter.
Meaning: Used to add another related point.
First example: I don't even like tea. Coffee, for that matter, either.
Second example: We don't have enough time, or resources for that matter.
Third example: Nobody understood him, not even his closest friends, for that matter.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

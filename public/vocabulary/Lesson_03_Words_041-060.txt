Vocabulary lesson 3. Words 41 to 60.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 41. Reluctantly.
Meaning: Doing something unwillingly.
First example: He reluctantly agreed to help.
Second example: She reluctantly apologized.

Word 42. Resentment.
Meaning: Bitter anger due to unfair treatment.
First example: His criticism created resentment among employees.
Second example: Holding resentment only harms you.

Word 43. Arguments.
Meaning: Disagreements or conflicts.
First example: They had many arguments about money.
Second example: Small issues often turn into big arguments.

Word 44. Necessarily.
Meaning: In a required or unavoidable way.
First example: Hard work doesn't necessarily guarantee success.
Second example: Expensive things aren't necessarily better.

Word 45. Adversity.
Meaning: Difficult situations or hardships.
First example: Great leaders often grow through adversity.
Second example: She stayed strong despite adversity.

Word 46. Diversion.
Meaning: Something that distracts or entertains.
First example: Watching a movie was a nice diversion from work.
Second example: Social media can become a big diversion.

Word 47. Chinese Parable.
Meaning: A short traditional Chinese story that teaches a lesson.
First example: My teacher shared a Chinese parable about patience.
Second example: Many Chinese parables contain deep wisdom.

Word 48. Double down.
Meaning: Continue strongly with a decision.
First example: The company decided to double down on its strategy.
Second example: Instead of quitting, he doubled down on his efforts.

Word 49. Fuzzy.
Meaning: Not clear or blurry.
First example: My memory of that event is fuzzy.
Second example: The picture looks fuzzy.

Word 50. Materialistic.
Meaning: Caring too much about money or possessions.
First example: He became too materialistic after getting rich.
Second example: A materialistic lifestyle doesn't guarantee happiness.

Word 51. Comprehend.
Meaning: To fully understand something.
First example: It took time to comprehend the problem.
Second example: Students must comprehend the concept before solving questions.

Word 52. Coping mechanism.
Meaning: A way people deal with stress or problems.
First example: Exercise is my coping mechanism when I feel stressed.
Second example: Some people use humor as a coping mechanism.

Word 53. Anecdotal evidence.
Meaning: Evidence based on personal stories, not scientific proof.
First example: That claim is based only on anecdotal evidence.
Second example: My experience is just anecdotal evidence, not real research.

Word 54. Accountable.
Meaning: Responsible for something.
First example: Leaders must be accountable for their decisions.
Second example: Everyone should be accountable for their work.

Word 55. Quote-unquote.
Meaning: Used when repeating someone else's words.
First example: He said he is “quote-unquote busy.”
Second example: She called herself a “quote-unquote expert.”

Word 56. Anecdotal.
Meaning: Based on personal stories rather than facts.
First example: His argument is mostly anecdotal.
Second example: The report includes anecdotal examples.

Word 57. Emulate.
Meaning: Try to copy or match someone’s success.
First example: Many entrepreneurs try to emulate successful leaders.
Second example: Children often emulate their parents.

Word 58. The Swiss.
Meaning: People from Switzerland.
First example: The Swiss are known for precision and quality.
Second example: Many people admire the neutrality of the Swiss.

Word 59. Unlike.
Meaning: Different from something else.
First example: Unlike last year, business is growing now.
Second example: Unlike him, I prefer working alone.

Word 60. Ingenuity.
Meaning: Cleverness and creativity in solving problems.
First example: The invention shows great ingenuity.
Second example: Human ingenuity solves many problems.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.

Vocabulary lesson 7. Words 121 to 140.
Listen to each word, its meaning, and the examples.
Word 121. Crevice.
Meaning: A narrow crack or gap.
First example: The plant grew from a crevice in the rock.
Second example: Water flowed through a small crevice.

Word 122. Adulterated.
Meaning: Mixed with something impure; not pure.
First example: The food was adulterated with harmful chemicals.
Second example: People worry about adulterated milk in some markets.

Word 123. Keen eye.
Meaning: Ability to notice small details.
First example: A designer must have a keen eye for detail.
Second example: She spotted the mistake with a keen eye.

Word 124. Apocalypse.
Meaning: A catastrophic end or disaster.
First example: The movie shows a world after an apocalypse.
Second example: Some people talk about climate change like an apocalypse.

Word 125. Bigotry.
Meaning: Intolerance toward people with different beliefs.
First example: Society must reject bigotry.
Second example: Education helps reduce bigotry.

Word 126. Out of the blue.
Meaning: Suddenly and unexpectedly.
First example: He called me out of the blue after years.
Second example: The opportunity came out of the blue.

Word 127. Consideration.
Meaning: Careful thought before deciding.
First example: After careful consideration, we accepted the offer.
Second example: Thank you for your consideration.

Word 128. Goofing around.
Meaning: Acting silly or joking.
First example: The kids were goofing around in the park.
Second example: Stop goofing around and focus on work.

Word 129. Appeal.
Meaning: Attraction or a request for support.
First example: The product has strong appeal to young buyers.
Second example: He made an appeal for help.

Word 130. Pile.
Meaning: A large stack of things.
First example: There is a pile of work on my desk.
Second example: Books were lying in a pile.

Word 131. Affirmative.
Meaning: Showing agreement or approval.
First example: She gave an affirmative response.
Second example: The answer was clearly affirmative.

Word 132. Argumentative.
Meaning: Always ready to argue.
First example: He becomes argumentative during debates.
Second example: Being too argumentative can harm relationships.

Word 133. Misperceive.
Meaning: Understand something incorrectly.
First example: People sometimes misperceive confidence as arrogance.
Second example: Don’t misperceive his silence as weakness.

Word 134. Distilled.
Meaning: Made pure or simplified.
First example: The book distilled complex ideas into simple lessons.
Second example: Wisdom often comes from distilled experience.

Word 135. Backhanded compliment.
Meaning: A compliment that actually insults.
First example: Saying “You’re smart for your age” can be a backhanded compliment.
Second example: His praise sounded like a backhanded compliment.

Word 136. Misperception.
Meaning: A wrong understanding of something.
First example: There is a misperception about this policy.
Second example: The media sometimes creates misperceptions.

Word 137. Assumption.
Meaning: Something you believe without proof.
First example: Don’t make an assumption without facts.
Second example: His decision was based on a wrong assumption.

Word 138. Introspection.
Meaning: Looking inward and thinking about your thoughts or actions.
First example: I spent some time in introspection after the failure.
Second example: Introspection helps you understand yourself better.

Word 139. Grazed.
Meaning: Lightly touched or slightly injured.
First example: I grazed my knee while playing football.
Second example: The bullet grazed his arm.

Word 140. Embodiment.
Meaning: A perfect example of something.
First example: She is the embodiment of kindness.
Second example: The athlete is the embodiment of discipline.

End of lesson. Review the words before moving on.
